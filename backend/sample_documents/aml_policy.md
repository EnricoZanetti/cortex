# Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Policy

Northgate Financial Services: Internal Policy NGF-COMP-004
Version 3.2 · Owner: Financial Crime Compliance · Review cycle: annual

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Northgate Financial Services identifies, prevents and reports
money laundering and terrorist financing. It applies to every employee, contractor and
introduced agent acting on behalf of the firm, in all jurisdictions where we operate.

Failure to follow this policy is treated as a disciplinary matter and may, in some
jurisdictions, constitute a personal criminal offence for the individual involved.

## 2. Customer Due Diligence

### 2.1 Standard due diligence

Before opening an account, the onboarding team must verify the client's identity using at
least two independent sources. Acceptable evidence is a government-issued photographic
identity document plus a proof of address dated within the last three months. For
corporate clients, we additionally verify the certificate of incorporation, the register
of directors, and the ownership structure up to the ultimate beneficial owners.

An ultimate beneficial owner is any natural person who directly or indirectly holds more
than 25% of the shares or voting rights, or who otherwise exercises control over the
management of the entity.

### 2.2 Enhanced due diligence

Enhanced due diligence is mandatory where any of the following applies:

- The client, or a beneficial owner, is a politically exposed person (PEP), a family
  member of a PEP, or a known close associate of a PEP.
- The client is resident in, or the funds originate from, a jurisdiction on the firm's
  high-risk country list.
- The relationship is conducted entirely non-face-to-face without a recognised digital
  identity provider.
- The expected annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 5,000,000.

Enhanced due diligence requires senior management approval before the relationship is
established, documented evidence of the source of wealth and the source of funds, and
review of the relationship at least every six months rather than annually.

### 2.3 Simplified due diligence

Simplified due diligence may be applied only to regulated financial institutions
supervised in an equivalent jurisdiction, and to listed companies on a regulated market
with full disclosure requirements. It never removes the obligation to monitor
transactions.

## 3. Ongoing monitoring

All client relationships are subject to automated transaction monitoring. The monitoring
system generates alerts against scenario rules that are reviewed quarterly by the
Financial Crime Compliance team.

Relationship managers must review and refresh client due diligence records on the
following cycle: high risk every 6 months, medium risk every 2 years, low risk every 5
years. A refresh is also triggered by any material change in the client's circumstances,
such as a change of beneficial ownership, a change of registered jurisdiction, or an
adverse media hit.

## 4. Reporting suspicious activity

### 4.1 Internal escalation

Any employee who knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds to suspect, that funds are
the proceeds of crime must submit an internal Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) to the
Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) **within 24 hours** of forming that suspicion.

Escalation is made through the Financial Crime portal. If the portal is unavailable, the
report must be sent by encrypted email to mlro@northgate.example and confirmed by phone.

### 4.2 Reporting thresholds

There is no monetary threshold for suspicion-based reporting: a suspicious transaction of
any value must be reported. Separately, the following transactions must be recorded and
reviewed regardless of suspicion:

- Any single cash transaction of EUR 10,000 or more.
- Any series of linked cash transactions totalling EUR 10,000 or more within seven days.
- Any wire transfer of EUR 15,000 or more to or from a high-risk jurisdiction.

### 4.3 Tipping off

Employees must never disclose to the client, or to any third party, that a SAR has been
made or that an investigation is under way. Doing so is a criminal offence known as
tipping off, and carries a maximum penalty of five years' imprisonment.

### 4.4 External reporting

Only the MLRO may file a report with the national Financial Intelligence Unit. The MLRO
must decide within three business days of receiving an internal SAR whether to escalate
externally, and must record the reasoning either way.

## 5. Record keeping

Due diligence records, transaction records and copies of all SARs are retained for five
years from the end of the client relationship or from the date of the transaction,
whichever is later. Records must be retrievable within 48 hours of a regulatory request.

## 6. Training

All staff complete AML training within two weeks of joining and annually thereafter.
Client-facing staff and staff in Financial Crime Compliance complete an extended module
covering typologies and case studies. Training completion is tracked by the Learning team
and reported to the Audit Committee each quarter.

## 7. Governance and breaches

The MLRO reports to the Chief Compliance Officer and has a direct escalation line to the
Board Risk Committee. Suspected breaches of this policy are investigated by Compliance
and reported to the Audit Committee. Employees may raise concerns anonymously through the
whistleblowing line, and the firm prohibits any retaliation against a person who reports
a concern in good faith.
